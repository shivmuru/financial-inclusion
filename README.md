# financial-inclusion
Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Summer 2024, Machine Learning

*Quantifying the Impact of Freedom on Financial Inclusion*, investigates how differences in political freedoms and governance quality influence financial inclusion across more than 100 countries. 

Our team comprised students from both Georgia Tech and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and we delivered our final presentation at the latter institution. 

Using data from the **World Bank Global Findex (2011–2021)** and the **Freedom House Index (2006–2024)**, we developed a composite **Financial Inclusion-Freedom Index (FIFI)** that integrates over 1,200 indicators of financial accessibility (such as account ownership, digital payments, and credit access) with 12 dimensions of political freedom, including civil liberties, rule of law, and political participation. 

We applied **regression and decision-tree models** to determine which freedoms most strongly predict inclusion outcomes, finding that civil liberties and rule of law consistently emerge as the most significant drivers of financial access. Our findings reveal a strong positive relationship between governance quality and financial inclusion, suggesting that strengthening institutional accountability and democratic freedoms can meaningfully improve access to formal financial systems worldwide.
